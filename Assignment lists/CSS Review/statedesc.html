Missouri is a Midwestern state known as the Show-Me State, a nickname that reflects its cultural identity of pragmatism and skepticism. It became the 24th state on August 10, 1821, entering the Union under the Missouri Compromise. Missouri sits at the geographic crossroads of the United States, serving historically as a gateway for westward expansion and exploration, including the Lewis and Clark Expedition that began in St. Louis
Geographically, Missouri is bordered by eight states, tying Tennessee for the most borders of any U.S. state. Its landscape includes the Ozark Mountains, the Mississippi River to the east, and the Missouri River running through its center. These natural features have shaped its development, transportation routes, and settlement patterns. Missouri covers roughly 69,700 square miles, ranking 21st in size among U.S. states.
Missouri's population is approximately 6.18 million, making it the 19th most populous state. Kansas City is its largest city, while St. Louis anchors the largest metropolitan area. The contrast between these two major cities—Kansas City's growth and St. Louis's population decline—highlights Missouri's diverse urban dynamics and regional identities.
Economically, Missouri has a gross state product of about $358 billion, supported by agriculture, manufacturing, transportation equipment, and tourism. The state's agricultural output includes soybeans, corn, cattle, and poultry, while its manufacturing sector produces vehicles, aerospace components, and food products. Tourism remains strong, with over 42 million visitors in FY2024.
Culturally, Missouri blends Midwestern and Southern influences. St. Louis and Kansas City are famous for their distinct styles of barbecue, jazz and blues traditions, and vibrant sports cultures. The state also has a rich historical legacy, from frontier exploration to Civil War tensions, and maintains numerous official symbols—including the rare Parrosaurus missouriensis fossil—reflecting its unique heritage.
Politically, Missouri's government is centered in Jefferson City, its capital. The state legislature, known as the General Assembly, includes a Senate and House of Representatives. Missouri's political landscape often mirrors national trends, with a mix of rural conservatism and urban progressivism shaping its elections and policies.
